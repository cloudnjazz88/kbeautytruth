Glass skin describes skin that looks clear, smooth, hydrated, and luminous enough to reflect light like glass. It is an appearance, not a skin type or a product you need to buy. In Korea, mulgwang—a dewy or “water-glow” look—may sound familiar, though the two terms are not necessarily used in exactly the same way.

## Why is glass skin so appealing?

The glow is easy to notice in a photo or short video. It makes for an appealing image and a memorable phrase on skincare packaging. Hydrating products, layered skincare, and makeup can all change how luminous skin looks, so two glass-skin photos may have very different routines behind them.

The appeal makes sense. Who wouldn’t want skin that looks fresh and comfortable? The tricky part is when a particular finish starts to feel like the measure of whether your skincare is working.

## Do you need a glass skin routine?

Not necessarily. A gentle cleanser, hydration, moisturizer, and daytime sunscreen can be a simple starting point; you do not need a special [ten-step routine](/trends/do-you-need-a-10-step-korean-skincare-routine/) to pursue a glow.

A shiny finish also does not tell the whole story of how skin feels. Someone may love a dewy look; someone else may prefer a lighter finish without feeling any less hydrated. Neither person needs to add more steps just to match a photo.

Enjoy the glass-skin look if you like it, but don’t treat it as a standard your skin has to meet. A routine should work for your skin and your life, whether the result looks glossy on camera or not.
