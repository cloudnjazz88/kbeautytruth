PDRN is everywhere in skincare right now. The way it’s talked about, you might expect a serum to erase wrinkles or make your skin look completely different. I was curious enough to try two products: medicube’s PDRN Pink Peptide Serum with Salmon DNA and its PDRN Pink Collagen Glow Jelly Mist Serum.

After roughly two months of using PDRN products, my honest answer is less dramatic than the hype.

## What is PDRN, and can a serum do what an injection does?

PDRN is made from fragments of DNA. You’ll see it in skincare products, but it’s also discussed in the context of treatments that place it into the skin. Those are different ways of delivering an ingredient. Results from an injection shouldn’t be treated as proof that a serum on the surface will do the same thing.

Getting PDRN through the skin barrier is a real question for topical products. Research on particular formulations has reported promising results, so I wouldn’t say that *no* PDRN cream can do anything. But a study of one formulation doesn’t tell me what every PDRN serum will do, or whether it will deliver the dramatic changes in an ad.

## My first month: PDRN products on their own

For about a month, I used the medicube PDRN products without a beauty device. They felt hydrating, and I liked that. But I couldn’t point to a clear change that made me think, *That must be the PDRN.* I didn’t notice dramatic wrinkle reduction or a sudden anti-aging transformation.

These are also formulas with other ingredients, not bottles of PDRN alone. Even the moisture I enjoyed can’t be credited to one ingredient with certainty.

## What changed when I added Booster Pro X2?

After I bought the medicube Booster Pro X2 and used it alongside the products, my skin seemed a little brighter. An area of discoloration also appeared to blend in better with the surrounding skin. It wasn’t an overnight makeover, but it was a change I noticed.

Using the device made the skincare feel as though it was working more deeply. **That feeling isn’t proof that PDRN reached a particular layer of my skin**, though. I can’t separate the effects of the device, the products, continued use, or the rest of my routine from my own observation.

## Kay’s take

I don’t regret trying these products. They gave me hydration, and I liked the modest change I noticed after adding the device. But I also think the PDRN hype played a part in why I bought them.

If you want a hydrating serum or mist, a PDRN product might still appeal to you. If you’re buying one expecting the same result as a procedure, dramatically fewer wrinkles, or guaranteed improvement in dark spots, I’d lower that expectation. **I enjoyed these products more as skincare than as a miracle in a bottle.**
