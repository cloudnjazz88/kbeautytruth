## The serum did more for my skin

If I’m judging them by the changes I noticed in my skin, I would give the serum the edge.

My skin tone looked better to me, and I felt it did more for the discoloration left after extractions. It also had a richer moisturizing feel. Between the two, the serum was the one that felt more nourishing on my slightly dry skin.

I wouldn’t turn that into a promise that it will fade someone else’s marks. I wasn’t testing these products under identical conditions, and I can’t separate every change from the rest of my routine. But when I think about which one seemed to do more for my skin, my answer is the serum.

That’s the part of this comparison I don’t want to lose just because I would personally buy the mist again first.

For the individual product details, read my [Medicube PDRN Pink Peptide Serum review](/reviews/medicube-pdrn-pink-peptide-serum-review/).

## The mist fit into my day more easily

The mist’s appeal was more immediate: glow, a little extra moisture, and being able to use it when I wanted a top-up.

I liked having that option over makeup, too. The serum felt more like a skincare step. The mist was something I could keep nearby and reach for during the day.

It didn’t feel as rich as the serum. If I were choosing only on the moisturizing feel, I would pick the serum. But the mist’s convenience meant I reached for it more often in everyday life.

That matters to me when I’m deciding what to repurchase. I can appreciate what a serum did for my skin and still prefer to spend my money on the product I use more readily.

## The spray is the mist’s biggest drawback

I like the mist, but I don’t like its spray enough to gloss over it.

It isn’t a fine, even cloud. The droplets are larger, and the spray doesn’t distribute evenly. That’s a noticeable drawback for a product whose convenience depends so much on spraying it on.

So when I say I prefer it for quick use, that doesn’t mean the nozzle is especially good. I like the glow and the way it fits into my day despite the spray.

My [Medicube Jelly Mist review](/reviews/medicube-pdrn-pink-collagen-jelly-mist-review/) includes the fuller account and the product video.

## Neither irritated my skin during my use

I didn’t notice irritation from either product.

My skin is slightly sensitive, so that was a positive part of using both. It’s also just my experience with these two products; it doesn’t tell me how someone else’s skin will respond.

## Which would I choose?

**For the skin changes I noticed and a richer moisturizing feel, I would choose the serum.** It came out ahead for my skin tone, the marks left after extractions, and that more nourishing feel.

**For immediate glow, moisture top-ups, and a product I could reach for over makeup, I would choose the mist.** Those were the reasons it became the more convenient option for me.

I don’t think using both is necessary just because they’re from the same pink PDRN range. The useful question is which of those roles you actually want in your routine.

If I could repurchase only one, I’d still choose the mist. I’d also still wish it had a better spray.

The serum wins on what I noticed in my skin. The mist wins on how often I want to use it.
